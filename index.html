5 · Investigate credits and calculate the refund · 7 min

A deduction lowers taxable income; a tax credit lowers calculated tax. A nonrefundable credit generally cannot reduce tax below zero. An eligible refundable credit can produce a refund even when no income tax was withheld.

Deduction$1,000 deduction in a 10% marginal bracket saves $100 in tax.
Credit$1,000 eligible nonrefundable credit against $1,500 in tax leaves $500 in tax.

Child Tax Credit (CTC)

In 2026, up to $2,200 per qualifying child under 17; the full amount is available below the phaseout threshold ($200,000 for an unmarried filer). Other rules include relationship, residence for more than half the year, dependent status, citizenship/residency and valid employment-authorized SSNs. Sofia is Marcus's daughter. Use his profile to decide.

IRS Child Tax Credit eligibility

Apply nonrefundable CTC up to the tax owed. The Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC) may refund unused credit, up to $1,700 per child, generally limited to 15% of earned income above $2,500. Marcus's earned income is high enough for the unused amount under this model.

Nonrefundable CTC = smaller of tax before credits and eligible CTC.
Remaining tax = tax before credits − nonrefundable CTC.
Refundable ACTC = smallest of unused CTC, $1,700 and 15% of earned income above $2,500.

Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)

For a qualifying unmarried worker with one qualifying child in 2026, the income limit is $51,593 and the investment income limit is $12,200. The maximum credit is $4,427; it decreases above $23,890 of income. Assume all other EITC conditions are met when the profile supports them (valid SSNs, U.S. residence, no conflicting claim). Payments, refundable credits and refund

This core model excludes other possible federal and Colorado credits, including the Saver's Credit. It uses statutory bracket calculations, not the final tax tables. Colorado income tax withholding and Social Security/Medicare do not count as federal income tax payments.
